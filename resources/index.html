Copper phone line retirement in Maryland and DC: a plain guide for small offices

The old copper phone lines many offices still rely on are being retired. Here is what that means and what to do about it, without the jargon.

By Rodrigue Dossouvi. Updated November 15, 2026.

What is happening

For decades, office phones ran over copper wire from the phone company to the building. Phone companies are now moving customers off copper and onto fiber and internet-based service.

On September 9, 2026, Verizon applied to the FCC to discontinue copper phone service in parts of Maryland and DC. The earliest date in that application is November 30, 2026, and it is pending approval. Not every address is affected at once, and customers are told their own date in a separate notice.

What is usually plugged into a copper line

Your main number

The line customers have called for years.

A fax machine

Still common in medical, legal and property offices.

An alarm or fire panel

It dials out over a phone line when something trips.

An elevator phone or door-entry system

Often installed years ago and forgotten until it stops working.

A card terminal or postage meter

Older models still dial out over a phone line.

Five things to do now

  1. Pull your last phone bill. List every line and number on it.
  2. Walk the office. Find what is plugged into each phone jack and write it down.
  3. Call your alarm and elevator companies. Ask whether your equipment needs a copper line and what they offer instead.
  4. Ask your phone provider for your date. Ask what they will replace each line with and whether your numbers stay the same.
  5. Decide what you no longer need. Many offices pay for lines nobody uses.

Common questions

What is a copper phone line?

It is the traditional phone line that runs over copper wire from the phone company to your building. Many offices still use one for a main number, a fax machine or an alarm panel.

Is copper phone service ending in Maryland and DC?

Verizon applied to the FCC on September 9, 2026 to discontinue copper phone service in parts of Maryland and DC, with November 30, 2026 as the earliest date, pending approval. Not every address is affected at once.

How do I know if my office has copper lines?

Look at your phone bill for lines listed as business lines or measured lines, and look for a phone jack feeding a fax machine, alarm panel or elevator phone. The free Network Age Check also asks about this.

What happens to my fax machine and alarm line?

They need a replacement path before the copper line goes away. Alarm, elevator and door-entry lines should be reviewed by the company that services them.
